## Summary

The Partnering Initiative (TPI) is a pioneer in the theory and practice of multi-stakeholder collaboration, founded in 2003. Unlike Catalyst 2030 or Harmonia, TPI is not a membership network — it is a specialist advisory and capacity-building organisation that helps other organisations design, broker, and manage cross-sector partnerships. Its Brokering Guidebook and 12-step partnership methodology have become widely used frameworks in the international development and corporate sustainability sectors. A recent impact review found that 87% of survey respondents said TPI engagement contributed to greater partnership impact through improved efficiency and stronger relationships.

---

## Key Model Elements

### Governance

TPI operates as an advisory organisation with a small expert team rather than a member-governed network. Governance is organisational (board-led) rather than participatory. This is a key structural difference from Harmonia — TPI governs itself, whereas Harmonia is governed by its members.

### Membership

TPI does not have a traditional membership model. It has a **network of practitioners** — individuals and organisations trained in partnership brokering. The network is built around shared methodology and professional development rather than binding commitments or fees.

### Revenue & Sustainability

Primarily earned revenue: consultancy fees, training programmes, and licensing of its methodology and tools. This makes TPI financially self-sustaining in a way that purely philanthropic networks are not — a model worth noting for Harmonia's earned revenue stream (`Training-and-Advisory`).

### Impact Measurement

TPI takes a rigorous approach to partnership evaluation. A 2023 working paper examined current practice in cross-sector partnership evaluation, and TPI's own impact review uses structured survey methodology. However, measurement is primarily at the partnership level (individual collaborations) rather than at a network level.

### Technology & Platform

No proprietary platform. TPI's tools and publications are largely open-access (Brokering Guidebook, 12 Steps framework, partnership diagnostic tools). The archive at `archive.thepartneringinitiative.org` hosts most of their methodology resources.

## Lessons for Harmonia

> [!note] Strategic relevance
> TPI's core contribution is the *methodology* of partnership brokering — a discipline Harmonia needs to embed in how it facilitates member connections, not just enables them.

| Element | What TPI does | Harmonia implication |
|---------|--------------|----------------------|
| **Partnership brokering as a discipline** | Has a named, trained role: the "partnership broker" — someone who manages the process of collaboration, not just the outcome | Harmonia's Chapter Coordinator role should include partnership brokering skills; consider training first cohort in TPI methodology |
| **Start small, scale up** | TPI advises partners to build trust through small initial collaborations before committing to larger ones | Harmonia's pilot projects should be small and bounded in Year 1 — proof of concept before ambitious cross-sector campaigns |
| **Co-created partnering agreement** | Every partnership gets a written agreement: roles, responsibilities, objectives, governance, accountability | Harmonia should provide a simple partnering agreement template for member-to-member collaborations (separate from membership pledges) |
| **The broker's neutrality** | The broker is not a participant — they facilitate and hold the process | Chapter Coordinators must be trained to broker without favouring specific members; especially important when NGO and corporate interests diverge |
| **Earned revenue via methodology** | TPI generates income through training and consultancy | Harmonia's `Training-and-Advisory` revenue stream should draw on partnership brokering training as a product |

## What's Different

TPI is a service provider; Harmonia is a network. TPI helps organisations broker partnerships; Harmonia creates the trust environment in which partnerships can form more readily. They are complementary, not competitive.

TPI's model does not involve binding member commitments — it operates through professional norms and trained competency, not accountability mechanisms. Harmonia's pledge model operates at the network level (all members commit to the same standards), whereas TPI's methodology operates at the partnership level (individual collaborations are structured through agreements).

> [!note] Potential partnership
> TPI's methodology is directly applicable to Harmonia's work. A formal relationship — training Harmonia Chapter Coordinators in partnership brokering, or licensing TPI tools for use within the network — could add significant value. Worth exploring.

---

## Criticisms and Limitations

- TPI's reach is limited by its advisory model — it works with a relatively small number of partnerships at depth, rather than building broad network infrastructure
- The methodology assumes a relatively neutral broker role, which can be difficult to sustain in practice when the facilitation body (like Harmonia) is itself a stakeholder in the network
- Academic literature on cross-sector partnerships (including Vogel 2022, Stadtler 2024) notes that the field lacks consistent evaluation standards — TPI is a leader but has not resolved this sector-wide challenge
