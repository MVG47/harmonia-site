## Overview

The cross-sector collaboration space — networks, coalitions, and platforms that bring NGOs, corporates, startups, and funders together — is crowded but shallow. Most structures facilitate connection without accountability. Harmonia enters a space where demand is real and infrastructure is inadequate.

---

## The State of Cross-Sector Collaboration

### What exists

The landscape broadly divides into four types:

| Type | Examples | What they do well | What they lack |
|------|---------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Movement networks** | Catalyst 2030, CIVICUS | Scale, reach, peer learning | Accountability; depth of collaboration |
| **Membership bodies** | Bond (UK), NCVO | Sector advocacy, professional community | Cross-sector engagement; binding commitments |
| **Accelerators/ESOs** | GrowthAfrica, Bethnal Green Ventures | Individual venture support | Network-level cross-sector infrastructure |
| **Methodology providers** | The Partnering Initiative | Partnership design expertise | Scale; member community |

None of these combine: (a) genuine cross-sector membership, (b) binding commitments with accountability, and (c) a federated chapter model that gives Global South chapters equal authority.

### The trust problem

Academic literature and practitioner experience consistently identify **trust** as the primary barrier to effective cross-sector collaboration (see Vogel 2022, Stadtler 2024 in academic literature; TPI's brokering practice in practitioner space). Organisations from different sectors have:

- Different timelines (NGO project cycles vs. corporate fiscal years vs. startup sprint cycles)
- Different accountability relationships (donors vs. shareholders vs. founders)
- Different languages for impact (outcomes vs. ROI vs. impact multiples)
- Legitimate concerns about being instrumentalised by more powerful partners

Most networks assume these differences can be overcome through goodwill and good events. Harmonia's hypothesis is that they can only be overcome through **structural trust** — verified commitments, shared standards, and visible accountability.

### Trends working in Harmonia's favour

- **ESG mainstreaming** — corporate ESG obligations are increasingly mandatory (TCFD, CSRD in Europe); corporates need verified NGO and startup partners, not just brand associations
- **Trust-based philanthropy growth** — the Trust-Based Philanthropy Project (US-originated, growing globally) is shifting funder norms toward unrestricted giving and genuine partnership; Harmonia's model aligns with this
- **Post-SDG urgency** — the rebranding of Catalyst 2030 to "Catalyst Now" signals a broader sector shift: the 2030 framework feels too slow; networks focused on immediate action are resonating
- **Global South equity** — a growing chorus of criticism of Northern-led development networks; co-equal governance with Global South chapters from day one is increasingly valued, not just nice-to-have
- **Startup-NGO convergence** — particularly in Kenya ("Silicon Savannah"), the line between tech startups and social impact is blurring; fintech, agritech, and healthtech startups are often doing what NGOs previously did, and vice versa

## Kenya Landscape Specifics

Kenya has one of Africa's most developed cross-sector ecosystems:
- 10,000+ registered NGOs
- Africa's leading tech startup hub (Nairobi)
- Active corporate ESG market (Safaricom, KCB, Equity Bank)
- Major international funder presence (Mastercard Foundation, Ford Foundation, FCDO)

**The gap:** These sectors work in proximity but rarely in structured, accountable partnership. The NGO sector and the startup ecosystem both have their own umbrella bodies (Kenya NGO Council, GrowthAfrica/iHub) but no cross-sector trust infrastructure.

## UK Landscape Specifics

The UK has:
- World's largest concentration of international development NGOs
- Active social enterprise movement (Social Enterprise UK, UnLtd)
- Mandatory ESG reporting pushing corporates toward verified partnerships
- Mature philanthropic sector increasingly interested in collaborative approaches

**The gap:** Bond serves NGOs. BITC serves corporates. Social Enterprise UK serves social enterprises. No organisation holds all four sectors in a single accountable network. The UK is well-organised within sectors and poorly connected across them.

---

## Harmonia's Position

Harmonia is not trying to out-scale Catalyst 2030 or replace Bond. It is filling a structural gap: a **trust network** that makes cross-sector collaboration verifiable, accountable, and genuinely co-equal between Global South and Global North chapters.

The pitch is not "another network." The pitch is "the infrastructure the sector has been missing."
