## Summary

Impact Hub is a global network of 120+ locally founded and operated impact innovation hubs, incubators, coworking spaces, and nonprofits across 65+ countries. Founded in 2005 in London, it has evolved into one of the most sophisticated federated network models in the social impact space. What makes Impact Hub distinctive is its **co-ownership governance model** — local hubs collectively own and govern Impact Hub Company (based in Austria), giving them genuine shared authority over global assets and standards. The network uses a peer-review mechanism for new hub applications, and local hubs operate with significant autonomy within shared protocols agreed by all members. It is the closest structural comparator to Harmonia's intended federated chapter model.

---

## Key Model Elements

### Governance

A genuinely democratic and co-ownership model:

- All local hubs collectively own **Impact Hub Company** (the global entity, based in Austria)
- Local hubs must comply with protocols and policies **voted on and approved by the full network** — bottom-up, not imposed top-down
- A co-leadership structure emerged in 2011 as the network transitioned from founder-led to collectively governed
- New hub applications go through a **peer-review mechanism** — existing hubs contribute to expansion decisions, not just a central committee
- "Translocal programmes" and shared platforms enable coordination without centralisation

This model took years to develop and required explicit transition from founder governance to member governance — a journey Harmonia should plan for from the outset (the Founding Council sunset clause does this).

### Membership

- **Hub members** — locally operating hubs (organisations, not individuals) that own and govern the network
- **Individual members** — people who use Impact Hub spaces and services; free global membership available
- Each local hub sets its own membership criteria for individuals and organisations in its space
- New hubs go through a peer-review process before joining the network

### Revenue & Sustainability

- **Local hubs** are primarily self-sustaining through membership fees, coworking space rental, events, and programming
- **Global entity** is funded through contributions from local hubs plus philanthropic partnerships
- No single funder dependency at the global level — financial resilience built through the distributed hub model
- Each hub's financial sustainability is local responsibility; the network provides brand and methodology, not financial guarantees

### Impact Measurement

- Impact Report 2024–2025 published
- Measurement appears to be primarily qualitative and hub-reported; no evidence of a standardised network-level KPI framework comparable to what Harmonia is building
- The network's impact claim rests on the aggregate of hub-level activities rather than documented network-effect impact

### Technology & Platform

- Shared digital platform for network connectivity, resource sharing, and translocal programming
- Local hubs operate their own management systems but connect through global tools
- No single proprietary impact measurement platform described publicly

## Lessons for Harmonia

> [!note] Strategic relevance
> Impact Hub is the most direct structural model for Harmonia's federated chapter approach. Its co-ownership model, peer-review expansion process, and transition from founder to member governance are all directly applicable.

| Element | What Impact Hub does | Harmonia implication |
|---------|---------------------|----------------------|
| **Co-ownership governance** | Local hubs collectively own the global entity; genuine shared authority | Harmonia should explore whether chapter legal entities could co-own or co-govern a central entity as the network matures (Phase 3 consideration) |
| **Peer-review for new hubs** | Existing hubs review and approve new hub applications | Harmonia's future chapter admission process should involve the Cross-Chapter Council (existing chapters) in reviewing new chapter proposals — already designed into the Chapter Model |
| **Bottom-up protocols** | Shared standards are voted on by the full network, not imposed centrally | Harmonia's constitutional amendment process (supermajority vote) is consistent with this; avoid letting the founding team retain veto power beyond Year 1 |
| **Local financial autonomy** | Each hub is financially self-sustaining; global entity doesn't subsidise local operations | Harmonia chapters should be designed to reach financial self-sufficiency through local membership fees — the global entity (if it emerges) should not be a dependency |
| **Founder → member governance transition** | Explicit transition in 2011; difficult but essential to the network's legitimacy | Harmonia's Founding Council sunset is non-negotiable; plan the transition ceremony and documentation explicitly, don't let it drift |

## What's Different

Impact Hub's primary product is **physical space and community** — coworking, events, incubation. Harmonia's primary product is **trust infrastructure and cross-sector collaboration facilitation**. Impact Hubs are primarily local communities with a global brand; Harmonia chapters are nodes in a cross-sector trust network with explicit accountability to each other.

Impact Hub does not have a binding pledge or accountability mechanism for members — participation is opt-in and market-driven (members pay for space and services they value). Harmonia's pledge model is structurally different: it creates obligations, not just opportunities.

Impact Hub also targets primarily social entrepreneurs and innovators; Harmonia's explicit cross-sector model (NGO + Corporate + Startup + Philanthropist) is broader and more structurally complex.

## Criticisms and Limitations

- The co-ownership model took years to develop and was not the original design — Impact Hub went through significant governance turbulence before reaching its current model (the SSIR article "How the Hub Found Its Center" documents this)
- Financial sustainability varies dramatically by hub; some local hubs have struggled or closed
- The network's global brand coherence is inconsistent — local hubs adapt heavily to local context, which can dilute the brand
- Individual membership is free and passive — there is no equivalent of Harmonia's binding pledge at the individual/organisational level

> [!warning] Key lesson from Impact Hub's history
> The transition from founder governance to member governance was not smooth and required deliberate design. Harmonia should study this transition closely and make the Founding Council sunset explicit, documented, and celebrated — not something that happens by drift.
