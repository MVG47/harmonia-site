## Summary

Catalyst 2030 (rebranded as **Catalyst Now** in 2024–25) is a global movement of social entrepreneurs, philanthropists, impact investors, corporates, and governments working together for systems-level social change aligned with the SDGs. Founded in January 2020 at the World Economic Forum by serial social entrepreneur Jeroo Billimoria, it has grown rapidly to over 6,170 individual members representing 4,250+ organisations across 156 countries. The rebranding from "2030" to "Now" reflects a philosophical shift toward urgency — the belief that the solutions exist and need connecting immediately, not by a deadline. It operates as a non-hierarchical movement with a General Assembly of all members, regional chapters, and thematic working groups.

## Key Model Elements

### Governance

A layered but non-hierarchical structure:

- **General Assembly** — all members; meets monthly online; sets the movement's direction
- **Governing Council** — develops and drives the strategic plan; co-chairs serve 2-year terms, selected for diversity in skills, gender, and lived experience
- **Four specialist councils** + a **legal-financial board** and **Secretariat**
- **Working Groups** — member-led groups for specific functions (Communications, Memberships, Collaborations, Funding)
- **Issue-Based Groups (IBGs)** — thematic communities around specific social issues
- **Regional/Country Chapters** — local leadership; ambitious target of 100 chapters, prioritising the Global South

No evidence of a binding pledge framework or formal accountability mechanism for members. The structure is participatory and movement-oriented rather than accountability-driven.

### Membership

- Open to: social entrepreneurs, social enterprises, philanthropists, impact investors, corporates, governments, multilaterals, and innovators
- **Admission criteria:** SDG alignment; knowledge of social entrepreneurship and systems change; track record of accelerating change (or readiness to develop it)
- No membership tiers publicly described; appears relatively open
- No binding commitments or pledges mentioned in public materials
- Member count: 6,170+ individuals / 4,250+ organisations (2024–25)

### Revenue & Sustainability

- Primarily philanthropic/grant-funded (supported by foundations including the Chandler Foundation)
- "Catalytic funding" — small grants given to chapters and members at scale-up inflection points needing a coordinator or specific project funding
- An Enabling Environment Department builds funder partnerships and advocates for flexible grantmaking
- No publicly described membership fee model; appears to rely on philanthropic income rather than member fees

### Impact Measurement

- Focuses on systems change outcomes; measurement approach is not publicly detailed
- Annual impact report published (e.g. Impact Report 2024–25 via Catalyst Now)
- Evidence base appears to be qualitative case studies and member stories rather than quantitative network-level KPIs

### Technology & Platform

- Web-based member directory and resource sharing
- Monthly online General Assembly (all-member video calls)
- Buddy System for peer matching
- No mention of a proprietary impact measurement or partner discovery platform

## Lessons for Harmonia

> [!note] Strategic relevance
> Catalyst is the closest comparable to Harmonia in mission and cross-sector scope. Its scale provides evidence that the cross-sector network model works — but its looseness highlights exactly where Harmonia must be different.

| Element | What Catalyst 2030 does | Harmonia implication |
|---------|------------------------|----------------------|
| **Urgency of positioning** | Rebranded from "2030" to "Now" because a deadline felt passive; urgency drives engagement | Harmonia's pitch should lead with urgency ("the infrastructure the sector is missing") not a future state |
| **Monthly all-member calls** | General Assembly meets monthly online; creates ongoing belonging | A monthly cross-chapter virtual touchpoint should be built into Harmonia's rhythm from Day 1 |
| **Chapter model for Global South** | Explicit ambition to create 100 chapters, prioritising Global South; catalytic grants for chapter coordinators | Validates Harmonia's Kenya-first approach; also signals that chapter coordinators need funded roles, not volunteer ones |
| **Working groups as engagement engine** | Members engage through issue-based groups and working groups, not just events | Harmonia should build its working group / thematic community structure in parallel with founding member recruitment, not after |
| **Admission openness** | Low barriers to joining; 6,170+ members | Harmonia's deliberately higher bar (binding pledges, screening) is a differentiator — but be clear in pitch materials that this is a choice, not a flaw |

## What's Different

Catalyst Now is a **movement**; Harmonia is a **trust network**. The difference is accountability. Catalyst has 6,170 members with no apparent binding pledge or graduated consequences — which makes it excellent for peer learning and connection but structurally unable to guarantee that members actually collaborate or deliver. Harmonia's binding pledge model addresses this gap directly.

Catalyst's membership criteria require alignment and knowledge, but not commitment to specific behaviours. Harmonia requires demonstrated commitments — resource sharing, active participation, transparent reporting — and has a mechanism to enforce them. This is Harmonia's clearest point of differentiation: membership means something verifiable.

Catalyst is also primarily philanthropically funded (no clear member fee model). Harmonia's tiered membership fee model is a deliberate sustainability choice — reducing dependence on grant funding.

---

## Criticisms and Limitations

- **Scale vs depth:** With 6,170 members across 156 countries, the network risks becoming a directory rather than a community. No public evidence of mechanisms to ensure genuine member engagement beyond self-selection.
- **No accountability layer:** Without binding pledges or consequences, there is no structural guarantee that members contribute to the network rather than extracting from it. This is the core limitation of the movement model at scale.
- **Grant dependency:** Without a clear membership fee model, the network's sustainability depends on continued philanthropic funding — which introduces vulnerability to funder priorities changing.
- **Measurement gap:** Impact reporting appears largely qualitative; it is unclear how Catalyst measures whether its members collectively achieve more than they would independently.
