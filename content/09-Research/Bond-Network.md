## Summary

Bond is the UK membership body for NGOs working in international development, uniting over 450 organisations — from small specialist charities to large INGOs like Oxfam and Save the Children. Founded as "British Overseas NGOs for Development," it is now simply Bond. It operates as a registered charity governed by an 11-trustee board elected from within the membership. Bond's primary value to members is collective advocacy (influencing UK government and policy-makers), peer learning (working groups, communities of practice, events), and sector intelligence. It is funded through a mix of member subscriptions, paid services, and grants (including FCDO and Gates Foundation). Bond is Harmonia's single most important access point for the UK NGO founding member pipeline.

---

## Key Model Elements

### Governance

- Registered charity with an 11-trustee board, generally elected from within the membership
- Member-governed in principle; board composition reflects the sector
- No explicit co-ownership or chapter model — Bond is a single UK entity
- Publishes a sector manifesto developed collaboratively with members (2024 Manifesto: "A Global System for People and Planet")
- Working groups are active and member-led; 2024 working group output was substantive across multiple thematic areas

### Membership

- **Full members:** UK-based NGOs working in international development; must be non-governmental, non-profit distributing, and non-partisan
- **Associate members:** Organisations not directly in development but supporting it (e.g. research, training bodies)
- **International Associate members:** Non-UK organisations, same terms as full members but different eligibility criteria
- 450+ member organisations; 1,600+ individuals from 300+ organisations engaged in working groups and communities of practice
- No binding pledges; membership is a professional affiliation rather than a commitment-based model
- No mention of accountability mechanisms for members

### Revenue & Sustainability

- **Member subscriptions** (primary earned revenue)
- **Paid-for services** (training, events, consultancy)
- **Grants** including FCDO and Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation
- This mixed model (earned + grant) is more sustainable than grant-only, but the FCDO grant creates some dependency on UK government priorities — relevant given current UK aid budget debates

### Impact Measurement

- Collective advocacy outcomes (policy influence, government engagement)
- Member engagement metrics (working group participation, event attendance)
- No publicly described network-level KPI framework for member impact

### Technology & Platform

- Member portal for resources and peer connection
- Working group collaboration tools
- Events platform
- No proprietary impact measurement or partner discovery platform

## Lessons for Harmonia

> [!note] Strategic relevance
> Bond is not a comparable in terms of model — it doesn't use binding pledges, cross-sector membership, or accountability mechanisms. Its relevance is as an **entry point** and **pitch reference point** for UK NGO founding members.

| Element | What Bond does | Harmonia implication |
|---------|---------------|----------------------|
| **Collective advocacy as a core value prop** | Members join primarily for collective voice with government | Harmonia should position collective advocacy as a benefit explicitly in the UK pitch — NGOs value it, and Harmonia's cross-sector composition gives advocacy more credibility than a sector-only body |
| **Working groups as the engagement engine** | 1,600+ individuals engage through working groups, not just events | Harmonia must launch working groups alongside or shortly after founding member recruitment — thematic communities of practice are how members experience the network day-to-day |
| **Mixed funding model** | Member fees + paid services + grants | Validate Harmonia's fee + training + grant funding model — this is proven at Bond's scale |
| **No binding pledge = differentiation** | Bond membership has no accountability requirements | This is the gap Harmonia fills. UK NGO prospects who are frustrated with networks where members don't actually collaborate will find Harmonia's pledge model compelling — use Bond's model as the "before" in the pitch |
| **FCDO relationship = warm door** | Bond has an FCDO grant relationship | FCDO may be an early funder for Harmonia UK; approach Bond about introductions once the network is formed |

---

## What's Different

Bond is sector-homogeneous (NGOs only); Harmonia is explicitly cross-sector. Bond is a national body (UK); Harmonia is a global network with equal chapters. Bond membership is professional affiliation; Harmonia membership is a binding commitment.

Bond is Harmonia's most important **outreach channel** in the UK, not its structural model. A co-hosted event with Bond or a profile in Bond's member communications would immediately reach the right UK NGO audience.

---

## Criticisms and Limitations

- Bond's focus is exclusively on international development NGOs — it doesn't facilitate cross-sector partnerships with corporates or startups
- Membership has no accountability layer — a Bond member who doesn't engage, collaborate, or contribute is treated the same as one who does
- Some members join primarily for the brand association and FCDO access rather than genuine peer learning
- FCDO funding creates vulnerability to UK aid budget fluctuations
