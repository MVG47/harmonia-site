## Purpose

This framework defines how Harmonia is governed — who makes which decisions, how authority is distributed across chapters, and how the network remains accountable to its members rather than its founders. It is designed for a trust network that must be genuinely equitable across sectors, geographies, and organisation sizes.

## Scope

This framework applies to all Harmonia members, chapters, and governance bodies from the date of adoption. It takes effect when ratified by the founding members of both the Kenya and UK chapters.

## Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Chapter** | A geographically-anchored member collective with its own legal entity, committee, and operational authority |
| **Chapter Committee** | The elected governance body of a single chapter |
| **Cross-Chapter Council** | The network-wide governance body with equal representation from all chapters |
| **Founding Council** | A time-limited stewardship body held by Harmonia's founders, with a clear sunset |
| **Constitutional matters** | Changes to Harmonia's core principles, founding pledges, or this governance framework |
| **Network decisions** | Decisions that affect all chapters (e.g. brand, membership criteria, shared platform) |
| **Chapter decisions** | Decisions within a chapter's own operations (e.g. local recruitment, events, partnerships) |

---

## Part 1: Governance Philosophy

Harmonia's governance reflects the same values it asks of its members. Three principles shape every structural decision:

**Collective ownership.** Governance belongs to members, not founders. The founders' role is to steward the network into existence — not to run it indefinitely. Every governance body has a clear path to member control.

**Equity across sectors and geographies.** No single sector — NGO, corporate, startup, or philanthropist — dominates any governance body. No chapter has more authority than another because it is larger or better-resourced.

**Transparency.** Governance processes are visible and explainable. Members can see how decisions are made, who made them, and why.

## Part 2: Governance Structure

Harmonia operates across three tiers of governance, each with defined authority and accountability.

## Part 3: The Founding Council

### Role
The Founding Council exists to get Harmonia off the ground — establishing governance, recruiting founding members, and transitioning authority to the Cross-Chapter Council. It is not a permanent body.

### Composition
- Harmonia's founders (up to 3 individuals)
- Operates during Phase 1 (Year 1) only

### Authority
During Phase 1, the Founding Council may:
- Make operational decisions required to establish the network
- Appoint the first Chapter Committees in consultation with founding members
- Set initial membership criteria and pledge language (subject to founding member ratification)
- Represent Harmonia externally before formal governance is in place

### Sunset provision

> [!important] The Founding Council dissolves at the end of Year 1
> No later than 12 months after Harmonia's formal launch, all governance authority transfers to the Cross-Chapter Council and Chapter Committees. Founders may stand for elected positions on those bodies, but have no automatic ongoing authority. This sunset is not negotiable — it is what makes Harmonia's governance genuinely member-owned.

## Part 4: Chapter Committees

### Composition
Each chapter committee has **6 elected seats**, distributed across member types to prevent any sector from dominating:

| Seat | Member type |
|------|-------------|
| 2 seats | NGO or Charity representatives |
| 2 seats | Corporate or Startup representatives |
| 1 seat | Philanthropist or Funder representative |
| 1 seat | At-large (any member type, elected by full chapter membership) |

No single sector may hold more than 2 seats at any time. If a vacancy creates an imbalance, it must be filled by the underrepresented sector.

### Election
- Elected annually by the full chapter membership (one member, one vote)
- Committee members serve one-year terms, renewable up to three times consecutively
- A chapter coordinator (paid staff role, not elected) supports the committee operationally

### Authority — Chapter Committees govern:
- Local member recruitment, screening, and onboarding
- Local programming, events, and partnerships
- Chapter budget and expenditure (within network-wide financial policies)
- Local accountability processes (Levels 1–3 of graduated consequences)
- Proposals to the Cross-Chapter Council on network-wide matters

### Quorum and voting
- Quorum: 4 of 6 committee members
- Routine decisions: simple majority of those present
- Significant decisions (e.g. suspending a member, committing >£/KES [threshold] in expenditure): 5 of 6 must approve

## Part 5: The Cross-Chapter Council

### Purpose
The Cross-Chapter Council governs matters that affect the entire network — things that must be consistent across chapters to maintain Harmonia's integrity and brand.

### Composition
- **3 representatives from each chapter**, regardless of chapter size or member count
- Each chapter's representatives must span at least 2 different member sectors
- Representatives are elected by their chapter committee from among active chapter members

This equal representation model is foundational. A chapter with 25 members has the same voice as a chapter with 8 members. Harmonia is not a network where resources equal influence.

### Meeting cadence
- Quarterly meetings (virtual), with an in-person annual convening
- Extraordinary meetings can be called by any chapter committee with 14 days' notice

### Authority — the Cross-Chapter Council governs:
- Network-wide membership criteria and standards
- The Unified Impact Platform and shared digital infrastructure
- Harmonia's brand identity and external communications standards
- Shared measurement framework and reporting requirements
- Cross-chapter financial flows and network-level budget
- Admission of new chapters beyond Kenya and UK
- Appeals at Levels 4–5 of graduated consequences
- Amendments to network-level governance documents (excluding constitutional matters)

### Quorum and voting
- Quorum: representation from all active chapters (currently 2 of 2)
- Routine decisions: simple majority of council members present
- Significant network decisions: two-thirds majority required
- Constitutional matters: see Part 6

## Part 6: Constitutional Matters

Changes to Harmonia's core principles ([[Core-Principles]]), founding pledges ([[Member-Pledges]]), or this governance framework are constitutional matters. They require:

1. Proposal submitted in writing by any chapter committee or 20% of network members
2. 60-day consultation period — all members may submit written responses
3. Discussion at a full network convening (in-person or virtual, all members invited)
4. Ratification by **two-thirds of all network members** across all chapters, via ballot

> [!note] Why such a high bar?
> The principles and pledges are what make Harmonia a trust network rather than just a membership organisation. They should only change when there is genuine, broad consensus — not when a majority of a single chapter prefers something different.

## Part 7: Future Chapters

Harmonia is designed to grow beyond Kenya and UK. New chapters join through the following process:

1. **Expression of interest**: A group of at least 5 organisations in a geography submits a chapter proposal to the Cross-Chapter Council
2. **Due diligence**: The Council reviews alignment with core principles, local legal feasibility, and chapter viability (minimum viable membership)
3. **Approval**: Two-thirds majority of the Cross-Chapter Council approves
4. **Formation**: The new chapter establishes its local legal entity and elects its committee within 6 months
5. **Representation**: The new chapter joins the Cross-Chapter Council with equal representation (3 seats) from the date of formal launch

New chapters do not have a probationary period of reduced authority — equal from day one.

---

## Part 8: Accountability of Governance Bodies

The governance bodies are themselves accountable to members:

- **Annual governance review**: Each year, the Cross-Chapter Council publishes a report on network-wide governance — decisions made, attendance records, financial summary
- **Member recall**: Any chapter committee member can be recalled by a two-thirds vote of that chapter's membership if they persistently fail their duties
- **Term limits**: No individual may serve on the Cross-Chapter Council for more than 6 consecutive years across all terms

---

## Review and Amendment

- **Review cycle**: Annually, and when a new chapter is admitted
- **Amendment process**: Amendments to this framework (other than constitutional elements) require a two-thirds majority of the Cross-Chapter Council
- **Owner**: Cross-Chapter Council (Founding Council prior to handover)

## Legal Note

> [!warning] Requires legal review
> This governance framework must be reviewed by solicitors in both the UK and Kenya to ensure it is compatible with the articles of association / constitution of each chapter's local legal entity. The governance framework does not replace those legal documents — it operates alongside them.
