## What a Chapter Is

A Harmonia chapter is a geographically-anchored community of members with its own legal entity, elected governance committee, and operational independence — within the shared framework that makes every chapter recognisably Harmonia.

The chapter model reflects a core conviction: genuine collaboration must be rooted in context. A Nairobi chapter and a London chapter will operate differently, engage different sectors, and focus on different thematic priorities. The chapter structure makes this possible while the cross-chapter framework ensures the network's principles and brand remain coherent.

---

## Co-Equal by Design

The most important feature of Harmonia's chapter model is **co-equality**. This means:

- No chapter has more authority over the network than another, regardless of its size or member count
- All chapters have equal representation on the Cross-Chapter Council (3 seats each, always)
- A UK chapter decision cannot be imposed on the Kenya chapter, and vice versa
- No chapter is a "pilot" for another — all chapters are full members of the network from day one

Co-equality is not just a principle — it is built into the governance structure. A network that claims to be global but is actually Northern-led is not the kind of network Harmonia intends to be.

## What Each Chapter Has

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Local legal entity** | Registered in-country (UK: Community Interest Company; Kenya: Company Limited by Guarantee or NGO). Each entity is independently accountable to its local legal and regulatory framework. |
| **Chapter Committee** | 6 elected members spanning all member sectors; governs local operations, recruitment, and programming |
| **Chapter Coordinator** | A paid staff role supporting the committee operationally — not an elected governance position |
| **Local membership** | Members recruited locally, onboarded locally, and accountable to the local committee |
| **Chapter budget** | Funded primarily by local membership fees and chapter-level grants; networked via shared financial policies |
| **Local programming** | Events, partnerships, and initiatives designed for the local context |

---

## What Is Shared Across All Chapters

The following are network-wide standards that every chapter commits to. They cannot be varied by individual chapters:

| Fixed element | Why it can't vary |
|---------------|------------------|
| **Core Principles** | The values every member signs up to — Transparency, Accountability, Collaboration, Equity, Impact First, Continuous Improvement |
| **Member Pledges** | The binding commitments every member makes — same across all chapters |
| **Brand and identity** | Harmonia's name, visual identity, and external positioning — must be coherent for the network to build trust globally |
| **Accountability standards** | The graduated consequences framework and minimum monitoring standards |
| **Shared platform** | All chapters use the Unified Impact Platform — enabling cross-chapter partner discovery |
| **Impact measurement framework** | Shared KPI definitions and reporting standards — so network-level impact can be measured |

## What Each Chapter Controls

Within the shared framework, chapters have full operational authority over:

- Which organisations to recruit and admit as members (subject to shared criteria)
- Local events, programming, and partnerships
- Thematic priorities for their chapter's work (reflecting local context)
- Chapter budget and expenditure (within network-wide financial policies)
- Local communication and outreach
- The composition and election of their chapter committee
- Proposals to the Cross-Chapter Council on network-wide matters

---

## How Chapters Communicate and Collaborate

Chapters are not independent silos — they are connected nodes in a single network. The architecture of connection:

**Cross-Chapter Council:** Meets quarterly; all network-wide decisions made here. Each chapter has 3 elected representatives.

**Cross-chapter member exchanges:** Members may attend events and participate in working groups in other chapters. The platform facilitates cross-chapter partner matching.

**Annual network convening:** All members from all chapters, once per year. In-person where possible. This is the primary moment for the network to be a network — not just a collection of chapters.

**Shared platform:** The Unified Impact Platform allows any member to discover and connect with any other member, regardless of chapter.

**Joint initiatives:** Chapters may co-design and co-deliver cross-border initiatives where their memberships and missions align.

## Adding New Chapters

Harmonia is designed to expand beyond Kenya and UK. The path to new chapter formation:

1. **Interest group** — a group of at least 5 organisations in a new geography expresses interest and submits a chapter proposal to the Cross-Chapter Council
2. **Feasibility review** — the Council assesses: local legal feasibility, sector diversity in the interested group, alignment with core principles, and minimum viable membership
3. **Council approval** — two-thirds majority of the Cross-Chapter Council
4. **Legal establishment** — the new chapter registers its local legal entity within 6 months of approval
5. **Committee election** — founding members of the new chapter elect their first committee
6. **Full integration** — the chapter joins the Cross-Chapter Council with 3 seats from launch day

**Principles for new chapters:**
- No probationary period with reduced authority — equal from day one
- The same Governance Framework applies — new chapters do not get to vary core governance
- New chapters are supported through the onboarding process by the existing chapters, not managed by them

---

## Sunset of Founding Status

Kenya and UK have a specific status as **founding chapters** — they shaped the governance, pledge language, and platform that all future chapters inherit. This founding status carries no ongoing additional authority over other chapters. When a third chapter joins, all three are equal.
