## Purpose

Accountability is what separates a trust network from a directory. This document defines how Harmonia monitors member commitment to their pledges, and what happens when members fall short. The approach is **supportive first, punitive last** — the goal at every stage is to help members get back on track, not to remove them.

## Scope

Applies to all Harmonia members from the date they sign their member pledges. Governance bodies are also subject to accountability provisions (see [[Governance-Framework]]).

---

## What Is Being Monitored

Accountability tracks compliance across the four pledge categories defined in [[Member-Pledges]]:

| Pledge | Key indicators monitored |
|--------|--------------------------|
| **Resource Sharing** | Evidence of in-kind contributions, response to resource requests |
| **Transparent Reporting** | Annual impact report submitted on time, completeness and honesty of content |
| **Active Participation** | Cross-sector initiative participation, convening attendance, working group engagement |
| **Cross-Sector Engagement** | Documented partnerships with members from different sectors |

## How Monitoring Works

Harmonia uses a layered approach — not surveillance, but structured visibility.

### Self-reporting (primary method)
Each member submits an annual impact report covering their pledge commitments. The report is reviewed by the Chapter Committee and shared (in summary) with the member's peer accountability circle.

> [!important] Annual report deadline
> All members must submit their annual impact report by **31 March** each year for the preceding calendar year. First reports are due 31 March following the first full year of membership.

### Peer accountability circles
Members are grouped into circles of 4–6 organisations across different sectors. Circles meet quarterly (or more often if they choose) to share progress, offer support, and flag concerns informally before they escalate. Circles are one of Harmonia's most important trust-building mechanisms — they are where real relationships form.

### Platform data
Where members use the Unified Impact Platform, engagement data (partnerships initiated, resources shared, contributions logged) supplements self-reporting. Platform data is shared with members — it is never used punitively without a member's opportunity to respond.

### Peer flags
Any member may raise a concern about another member's pledge compliance directly with the Chapter Committee. Flags are investigated before any action is taken — a flag is not a sanction.

## The Graduated Response Ladder

When concerns arise, Harmonia responds in proportion to the seriousness and persistence of the issue.

### Level 1 — Friendly Reminder

**Trigger:** A missed deadline, unfulfilled commitment, or first-time concern — with no prior issues.

**Who responds:** Chapter Coordinator

**What happens:**
- A direct, non-formal message acknowledging the missed item and asking if support is needed
- No record on the member's formal accountability file
- Resolved when the commitment is fulfilled or a plan is agreed

**Tone:** Assume good faith. Most Level 1 situations are caused by capacity issues, not disengagement.

---

### Level 2 — Support Check-in

**Trigger:** A second missed deadline, persistent shortfall on participation, or failure to respond to a Level 1 reminder.

**Who responds:** Chapter Committee (delegated to one committee member)

**What happens:**
- A scheduled one-to-one check-in (video or in-person) within 30 days
- Discussion of what support the member needs to meet their commitments
- A simple, written support plan agreed and recorded (not punitive — practical steps)
- Follow-up in 60 days

**The member's file:** The check-in and support plan are noted internally, not shared with the wider membership.

---

### Level 3 — Formal Notice

**Trigger:** Failure to engage with Level 2, persistent non-compliance across multiple pledge areas, or a single serious breach of the principles (e.g. a significant transparency violation).

**Who decides:** Chapter Committee (two-thirds vote required)

**What happens:**
- A written formal notice sent to the member's senior leadership (not just the day-to-day contact)
- Notice clearly states the specific concerns, what is needed to resolve them, and the timeline (90 days)
- The member has 14 days to respond in writing before the notice is finalised
- If the member disputes the basis of the notice, they may invoke the [[Conflict-Resolution-Process]]
- The member may not be publicly sanctioned at this stage — this is still an internal process

**Appeal:** The member may appeal the formal notice to the Cross-Chapter Council within 14 days of receiving it.

---

### Level 4 — Suspension

**Trigger:** Failure to meet the remediation plan set in the formal notice, or an exceptionally serious breach requiring immediate action (e.g. public conduct that actively damages Harmonia or a fellow member).

**Who decides:** Cross-Chapter Council (two-thirds majority)

**What happens:**
- Temporary suspension of the member's access to network benefits: platform access, referrals, and participation in initiatives
- The suspension is communicated to all chapter members in factual terms (no detail beyond "Member X is currently suspended pending a review")
- Suspension lasts up to 90 days while the member addresses the underlying issues
- A suspended member may continue to fulfil obligations to fellow members they are already partnering with — suspension is about network benefits, not punishing third parties

**Appeal:** The member may appeal to a panel of three members not involved in the original decision. The panel's recommendation is advisory to the Cross-Chapter Council.

---

### Level 5 — Removal

**Trigger:** Failure to resolve the concerns during suspension, or a breach so fundamental that continued membership would undermine the trust network's integrity.

**Who decides:** Cross-Chapter Council (two-thirds majority), following an appeal period

**What happens:**
- The member is formally removed from Harmonia
- Their member profile is removed from the platform and directory
- Any ongoing collaborative initiatives with other members are wound down with a 60-day transition period to minimise disruption to partners
- Removal is communicated to members factually: "Organisation X is no longer a Harmonia member"
- The removed organisation may reapply after 24 months, subject to a full new onboarding process

**Appeal:** Before the final vote, the member has 21 days to make a written case to the Cross-Chapter Council. The Council must consider it before voting.

## Summary Table

| Level | Trigger | Who acts | Member right to respond | Duration |
|-------|---------|----------|------------------------|----------|
| 1 — Reminder | First miss | Coordinator | Informal | Resolved on compliance |
| 2 — Check-in | Repeat miss | Committee member | Discussion in check-in | 60-day follow-up |
| 3 — Formal Notice | Persistent / serious | Chapter Committee | Written response + appeal | 90-day remediation |
| 4 — Suspension | Remediation failure | Cross-Chapter Council | Appeal panel | Up to 90 days |
| 5 — Removal | Fundamental breach | Cross-Chapter Council | Written case + 21 days | Permanent (reapply after 24 months) |

## Protecting Against Misuse

Accountability mechanisms can be weaponised — used to target competitors or settle interpersonal disputes. Harmonia guards against this by:

- Requiring two-thirds majorities for Levels 3–5
- Giving members the right to respond before any formal action
- Ensuring appeal panels exclude anyone involved in the original process
- Requiring the Cross-Chapter Council (not a single chapter) to make the most serious decisions

Any member who raises a complaint found to be made in bad faith may themselves face accountability review.

## Review and Amendment

- **Review cycle**: Annually, and following any Level 4 or Level 5 case
- **Amendment process**: Two-thirds majority of Cross-Chapter Council
- **Owner**: Cross-Chapter Council
