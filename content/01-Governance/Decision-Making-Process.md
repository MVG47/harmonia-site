## Purpose

This document defines how decisions are made across Harmonia — at chapter level, network level, and for matters requiring the highest threshold of consensus. Clear, fair decision-making processes are foundational to the trust the network depends on.

## Scope

Applies to all Harmonia governance bodies and member votes from the date of adoption.

## Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Operational decision** | Day-to-day running — events, communications, staff, partnerships within approved scope |
| **Strategic decision** | Significant choices affecting the network's direction, finances, or membership standards |
| **Constitutional decision** | Changes to core principles, founding pledges, or the governance framework |
| **Consensus** | All participants indicate they can accept a proposal (not necessarily their first preference) |
| **Simple majority** | More than half of votes cast |
| **Two-thirds majority** | At least 66% of votes cast |
| **Supermajority** | Two-thirds of all eligible voters (not just those present) |

## Decision Tiers

### Tier 1 — Operational decisions (Chapter Committee authority)

These decisions are made by the Chapter Committee and do not require wider member input.

**Examples:**
- Scheduling events and convenings
- Approving minor expenditure within the chapter budget
- Day-to-day communications and partnerships
- Administrative membership updates

**Process:** Chapter Coordinator proposes → Chapter Committee approves by simple majority at any quorate meeting.

**Quorum:** 4 of 6 Chapter Committee members.

---

### Tier 2 — Strategic decisions (Chapter or Cross-Chapter Council authority)

Decisions that set direction, commit significant resources, or affect membership.

**Chapter-level strategic decisions include:**
- Admitting or rejecting a member application
- Initiating or ending a formal chapter partnership
- Spending above the chapter's operational threshold (amount set annually)
- Suspending a member (Levels 3–4 of graduated consequences)

**Process:** Proposal circulated to Chapter Committee 7 days before meeting → Committee discusses → two-thirds majority required.

**Cross-Chapter Council strategic decisions include:**
- Changes to network-wide membership criteria
- Network-level budget and financial commitments
- Platform changes affecting all members
- Admitting a new chapter
- Removing a member at Level 5

**Process:** Proposal submitted to Cross-Chapter Council Secretariat → circulated to all council members 14 days before meeting → council discusses → two-thirds majority of council required.

---

### Tier 3 — Constitutional decisions (All-member vote)

Changes to Harmonia's core principles, founding pledges, or governance framework.

**Process:**
1. Proposal submitted in writing by a Chapter Committee or 20% of network members
2. 60-day consultation — all members may respond in writing
3. Presented at a full network convening (all members invited)
4. Ratification by **two-thirds of all network members** (not just those present at the convening), via a formal ballot open for 14 days

**Quorum for constitutional change:** No quorum — the ballot is open to all members. Two-thirds of all members must vote yes for the change to pass.

---

## Consensus-First Approach

For Tier 1 and Tier 2 decisions, governance bodies should seek consensus before calling a formal vote. Consensus means every participant can accept the proposal, even if it isn't their preferred option.

**How to reach consensus:**
1. Raise the proposal and invite questions — separate understanding from agreement
2. Identify any genuine objections (not just preferences) — an objection is a concern that the proposal would actively harm the network or a member
3. Modify the proposal to address objections if possible
4. If consensus cannot be reached after genuine effort, call a formal vote

Votes should not be called prematurely. A governance body that defaults to voting over dialogue will erode the trust the network is built on.

---

## Urgent Decisions

Occasionally, decisions must be made between scheduled meetings. The process for urgent decisions:

1. The Chapter Coordinator or Council Secretariat circulates the proposal to the relevant body by email, flagging it as urgent
2. A response deadline of 72 hours is set (or longer if the decision allows)
3. Members respond by email with approval, objection, or abstention
4. Simple majority of responses constitutes approval for operational decisions; two-thirds for strategic decisions
5. The decision is ratified at the next scheduled meeting and recorded in the minutes

> [!important] Urgent decisions are the exception
> If urgent decisions are becoming frequent, it is a signal that meeting cadence or delegation of authority needs review — not that urgent process should become normal.

---

## Conflicts of Interest

Any governance body member with a personal, financial, or organisational interest in a decision must:
1. Declare the conflict at the start of the relevant discussion
2. Withdraw from the discussion and the vote on that item
3. Have the declaration recorded in the minutes

The body should proceed to decision without the conflicted member's vote.

---

## Recording Decisions

All decisions made by Chapter Committees and the Cross-Chapter Council must be:
- Recorded in meeting minutes distributed to all participants within 5 working days
- Significant decisions logged in [[Decision-Log]]
- Published to the relevant members in a summary communication within 14 days of the meeting

Transparency in decision-making is not optional — it is what allows members to trust the governance they have collectively agreed to.

---

## Deadlock

If the Cross-Chapter Council cannot reach a decision after two attempts:

1. A mediation process is convened — each chapter nominates one member not currently on the Council to form a mediation panel
2. The panel has 30 days to propose a resolution
3. If the mediation panel's proposal is accepted by two-thirds of the Council, it passes
4. If deadlock persists, the decision is referred to an all-member ballot

> [!note] Deadlock is rare when the process is right
> Most deadlocks arise from poor framing, not genuine irreconcilable differences. The mediation step is designed to reframe — not simply to force a vote.
