## Who this is for

Harmonia's NGO and charity membership is open to registered non-governmental organisations and charities of all sizes — from local community groups to large international bodies. What matters is not scale but genuine commitment to social mission and the willingness to collaborate, not just network.

---

## What NGOs and Charities Bring to Harmonia

NGOs and charities are the backbone of the trust network. Without them, corporate CSR has no credible implementation partners, startups have no community knowledge, and philanthropists fund in a vacuum.

Specifically, they bring:
- **Grassroots knowledge** — understanding of communities, contexts, and needs that no corporate or tech company can replicate
- **Implementation capacity** — proven ability to deliver programmes on the ground
- **Community trust** — relationships with beneficiaries and local institutions built over years
- **Sectoral credibility** — accountability to donors, boards, and regulators that gives them moral authority in cross-sector partnerships
- **Mission clarity** — orientation toward social outcomes, not profit

## What Harmonia Offers NGOs

| Benefit | What it means in practice |
|---------|--------------------------|
| **Partner discovery** | Find vetted corporate, startup, and philanthropist partners aligned with your thematic area — without the cold outreach |
| **Resource access** | Access to shared services, technology, in-kind contributions, and pooled funding mechanisms |
| **Capacity building** | Training, peer learning, and access to business, legal, and technology expertise from corporate and startup members |
| **Collective voice** | Participate in coordinated advocacy alongside a network of cross-sector organisations |
| **Reduced duplication** | Coordinate with peer NGOs to avoid duplicating programmes and compete less for the same grants |
| **Legitimacy for funders** | Membership in a verified trust network signals accountability to donors and foundations |

## Admission Criteria

NGOs applying for Harmonia membership must demonstrate:

1. **Legal registration** — registered as an NGO, charity, CBO, or equivalent in their country of operation
2. **Active programming** — currently delivering or supporting active social impact programmes (not dormant)
3. **Transparency track record** — able to share annual reports, accounts, or equivalent documentation
4. **Pledge capacity** — realistic ability to fulfil the four membership pledges, proportional to organisational size

> [!note] Small and grassroots organisations
> Smaller organisations are actively welcomed. Pledge obligations are assessed proportionally — a 3-person NGO is not expected to contribute at the same scale as a 500-person INGO. What matters is genuine engagement, not volume of contribution.

## What We Ask of NGO Members

In addition to the standard [[Member-Pledges]], NGO members are specifically asked to:

- **Share implementation knowledge** openly with the network — what works, what doesn't, and why
- **Engage authentically with corporate members** — not as a transactional CSR vehicle, but as genuine partners where there is real alignment
- **Represent community interests** in cross-sector discussions — your proximity to beneficiaries is your most valuable contribution to the network

---

## Sector Balance Note

NGOs and charities are the largest membership group in Harmonia by design (target: 35–45% of each chapter). This reflects their centrality to the network's social mission. However, governance seats are distributed to prevent NGO dominance of decision-making — see [[Governance-Framework]].
