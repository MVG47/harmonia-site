## Who this is for

Harmonia's philanthropist membership is for individual philanthropists, family offices, and foundations that fund social impact work — particularly those interested in collaborative, systems-change, or trust-based approaches to giving. This includes both established foundations and individuals who are actively increasing their philanthropic engagement.

This is not for passive donors. Philanthropist members are in the network as active participants — contributing funding wisdom, connecting grantees, and co-designing collaborative funding mechanisms alongside other members.

---

## What Philanthropists Bring to Harmonia

Philanthropic members provide something no other member type can:

- **Unrestricted capital** — the kind of funding that enables NGOs and startups to do what's needed, not just what's reportable
- **Risk tolerance** — foundations and individuals can fund experiments, pilots, and failures in ways institutional funders cannot
- **Strategic convening power** — funders open doors that other members can't
- **Network capital** — connections to other funders, government, and international bodies
- **Long-term perspective** — philanthropic timelines can extend beyond typical grant cycles, enabling sustained investment in systems change

## What Harmonia Offers Philanthropists

| Benefit | What it means in practice |
|---------|--------------------------|
| **Vetted implementing partners** | A directory of verified NGOs, startups, and collaboratives — due diligence already done |
| **Collaborative funding opportunities** | Co-investment with other philanthropist members in initiatives that no single funder can resource alone |
| **Impact evidence** | Access to the network's shared measurement framework — understand what's working across a portfolio, not just your own grants |
| **Trusted intermediary** | Harmonia as a structure for pooled funding — reducing transaction costs and increasing coordination across grantees |
| **Field intelligence** | Insight from NGO and startup members on what the sector actually needs (not what funders assume it needs) |
| **Peer learning** | Connection with other philanthropist members working on similar challenges |

## Admission Criteria

Philanthropist applicants must demonstrate:

1. **Active giving** — currently making grants or social investments, or planning to do so within 12 months
2. **Mission alignment** — giving priorities that align with at least one of Harmonia's thematic areas
3. **Trust-based orientation** — openness to collaborative, unrestricted, and trust-based approaches (or willingness to learn). Harmonia is not a transactional grant-making directory.
4. **Engagement intent** — commitment to be present in the network, not just a funder looking at it from a distance

---

## Trust-Based Philanthropy

Harmonia actively promotes trust-based philanthropy practices among its philanthropist members — not as a requirement, but as an aspiration the network embodies:

- **Multi-year commitments** over one-off grants where possible
- **Unrestricted or lightly-restricted funding** that gives grantees flexibility
- **Listening before prescribing** — letting NGO members define their own priorities
- **Transparency about funding criteria** — no mysterious processes; NGOs know what they're applying for and why
- **Relationship, not transaction** — funders and grantees as genuine partners

> [!note] Why this matters
> Trust-based philanthropy is more effective at scale — but it requires funders to be present in the same space as their grantees, building real relationships. Harmonia creates that space.

## Collaborative Funding Mechanisms

Harmonia creates structured pathways for philanthropist members to pool resources for greater impact:

- **Challenge funds** — pooled grants for specific cross-sector initiatives, co-designed with NGO and startup members
- **Matching rounds** — members pledge funding that is matched or amplified by other philanthropists
- **In-network grants** — simplified grants to NGO members vetted by the network (reduced due diligence burden)

Participation in these mechanisms is voluntary. They are developed through the Cross-Chapter Council with input from philanthropist members.

---

## What We Ask of Philanthropist Members

In addition to the standard [[Member-Pledges]], philanthropist members are specifically asked to:

- **Be transparent about your funding criteria** — NGO and startup members need to know what you fund, what you don't, and why
- **Engage across the network** — not just with your current or prospective grantees. The network works when funders engage with everyone, not only those they're considering supporting.
- **Share knowledge, not just capital** — your experience of what works across a portfolio is valuable to the whole network
