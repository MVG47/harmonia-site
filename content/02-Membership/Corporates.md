## Who this is for

Harmonia's corporate membership is for companies that take social impact seriously — not as reputation management, but as something genuinely integrated into how they operate. This is not a CSR badge. It is a commitment to work alongside NGOs, startups, and philanthropists as genuine partners.

Corporate members may be large multinationals, regional businesses, or mid-size companies. What matters is whether the organisation's social impact commitment is real and whether senior leadership is behind it.

---

## What Corporates Bring to Harmonia

Corporate members are not in Harmonia to lend their logo. They bring tangible assets the rest of the network needs:

- **Financial resources** — discretionary funding, corporate foundations, in-kind budgets
- **Business acumen** — strategic thinking, operational expertise, supply chain knowledge
- **Technology and R&D** — access to tools, platforms, and innovation capabilities
- **Networks and reach** — customer bases, supplier relationships, government access
- **Scale** — ability to take successful pilots and help implement them at scale
- **Legitimacy with government and regulators** — corporate involvement signals viability and sustainability to policy-makers

## What Harmonia Offers Corporates

| Benefit | What it means in practice |
|---------|--------------------------|
| **Vetted implementing partners** | Access to a directory of verified NGOs and charities — no cold searching, no reputational risk from unknown partners |
| **Credible impact measurement** | Shared measurement framework and reporting standards that satisfy ESG and investor disclosure requirements |
| **Cross-sector innovation** | Structured pathways to co-develop solutions with startups and NGOs — ideas your own R&D wouldn't surface |
| **Workforce engagement** | Skills-based volunteering opportunities with mission-aligned organisations that staff actually want to support |
| **Collective advocacy** | Join a coalition voice on policy issues that affect both social outcomes and your operating environment |
| **Reputational trust** | Membership in a verified trust network, not a self-reported CSR claim |

## Admission Criteria

Corporate applicants must demonstrate:

1. **Genuine social impact commitment** — evidence of active CSR/ESG strategy with senior-level accountability, not a communications add-on
2. **No conflicts of interest** that would compromise the network's integrity or place NGO members in an uncomfortable position
3. **Ring-fenced engagement model** — the commercial and the social impact arms of the business must be clearly separated within the partnership; Harmonia is not a sales channel
4. **Senior sponsor** — a named senior leader (Director level or above) who is the internal champion for Harmonia membership

> [!important] What corporate membership is not
> Harmonia is not a PR platform, a CSR supplier directory, or a way to access NGO relationships for commercial data. Corporate members who treat the network transactionally will be flagged through the accountability process.

---

## The Ring-Fenced Model

To protect NGO members' trust, corporate members agree to keep their commercial and Harmonia engagement clearly separated:

- Harmonia partnerships may not be used to generate commercial leads without the explicit agreement of the NGO or startup partner
- Commercial offers to fellow members must be disclosed as such — not dressed as collaboration
- Corporate members may not use member data for commercial targeting

This isn't about limiting corporate engagement — it's about making sure NGO members feel safe enough to collaborate openly.

---

## What We Ask of Corporate Members

In addition to the standard [[Member-Pledges]], corporate members are specifically asked to:

- **Contribute meaningfully, not just financially** — expertise, secondments, and technology access are as valuable as cash
- **Engage across sectors genuinely** — attend events, join working groups, and make real connections rather than attending to be seen
- **Lead on climate and equity commitments** — corporate members should demonstrate that their social engagement is consistent with how they run their business
