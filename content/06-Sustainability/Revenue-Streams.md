## Overview

Harmonia is designed to be financially self-sustaining by Phase 3 (Year 5). The revenue model deliberately diversifies across 5 streams to avoid dependence on any single source — particularly philanthropic grants, which are subject to funder priority shifts.

The goal by end of Phase 3: 95%+ of operating costs covered by membership fees and earned revenue combined. Grants fund innovation, expansion, and specific programmes — not core operations.

---

## The Five Revenue Streams

### 1. Tiered Membership Fees (primary — Phase 1 onwards)

The core revenue stream. Fees are scaled to organisational size and type, with the principle that larger/wealthier organisations pay more so that smaller organisations can participate fully.

| Band | Annual fee (indicative) |
|------|------------------------|
| Grassroots | Subsidised / by arrangement |
| Small | £500–£1,500 / KES 75k–225k |
| Medium | £1,500–£5,000 / KES 225k–750k |
| Large | £5,000–£15,000 / KES 750k–2.2M |
| Corporate | £5,000–£25,000 / KES 750k–3.75M |
| Philanthropist | £2,000–£10,000 / KES 300k–1.5M |

Founding member discount: 25% in Year 1.

**Phase 1 projection (rough):** 15–25 founding members averaging £3,000 = £45,000–£75,000. This is not sufficient to cover all operating costs — grants and in-kind contributions are needed in Year 1.

**Phase 3 projection:** 150–250 members averaging £4,000 = £600,000–£1,000,000. At this scale, membership fees cover core operations.

### 2. Corporate Partnership Contributions (Phase 1 onwards)

Corporate members contribute above and beyond their membership fee through:
- Sponsorship of specific Innovation Hub cohorts or network events
- Contribution to pooled funds for NGO and startup members
- In-kind contributions (technology, staff time, venue) valued and recorded as financial equivalents

These contributions are voluntary and not a condition of membership — they are an expression of corporate members' commitment to the network's mission.

### 3. Earned Revenue — Training and Advisory (Phase 2 onwards)

See [[Training-and-Advisory]] for detail. External workshops, consultancy, and methodology licensing for non-members who want to build similar cross-sector collaboration infrastructure.

Target: 15–25% of total revenue by Phase 3.

### 4. Philanthropic Grants (Phase 1 heavy, reducing over time)

In Phase 1 and early Phase 2, philanthropic grants from foundations are essential to bridge the gap between membership fee income and operating costs.

**Target grant funders:**
- UK: FCDO (especially for Kenya-UK development collaboration angle), Esmée Fairbairn, Paul Hamlyn Foundation, Lloyds Bank Foundation
- Kenya: Mastercard Foundation, Ford Foundation, FCDO Kenya programme
- Global: Chandler Foundation (supports cross-sector network building), Open Society Foundations

Grant strategy:
- Seek multi-year grants (3-year commitments) rather than annual
- Frame around: filling the trust infrastructure gap in cross-sector collaboration; the Kenya-UK co-equal chapter model as a proof of concept
- Reduce grant dependency progressively: grants = 60% of income in Year 1, 30% by Year 3, 10% by Year 5

### 5. In-Kind Contributions (Phase 1 onwards)

Non-financial contributions that reduce cash costs:
- Technology: platform hosting, tools, software licences from tech company members
- Pro bono legal and financial services from professional services corporate members
- Venue and event hosting from corporate or philanthropist members
- Staff secondments from large member organisations to support Chapter Coordinator capacity

In-kind contributions are valued, logged in the platform (as resource sharing), and counted toward the network's resourced mobilisation KPI.

## Financial Sustainability Milestones

| Phase | Target |
|-------|--------|
| End of Phase 1 (Year 1) | 40% of operating costs from membership fees; remainder from grants and in-kind |
| End of Phase 2 (Year 3) | 70% from membership fees + earned revenue; 30% from grants |
| End of Phase 3 (Year 5) | 90–95% from membership fees + earned revenue; grants for innovation only |
