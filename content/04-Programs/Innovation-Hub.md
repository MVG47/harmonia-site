## What the Innovation Hub Is

The Innovation Hub is Harmonia's primary cross-sector programming offer — the structured space where startups, NGOs, and corporates actively co-develop solutions to social challenges. While the rest of the network facilitates organic partnership, the Innovation Hub creates structured, time-bounded collaboration with specific outputs.

The Innovation Hub launches in Phase 2 (Year 2). Phase 1 creates the member relationships that make it viable.

---

## The Three Formats

### 1. Challenge Briefs
An NGO member identifies a specific technology or capability gap in their work and posts a **challenge brief** to the network:

- **Who posts:** NGO or charity members
- **What it contains:** The problem, the context, what kind of solution they're looking for, what they can offer in return (co-design access, field testing, letters of support, modest budget)
- **Who responds:** Startup members pitch their solution; corporate members can offer to fund or co-develop

Challenge briefs are reviewed by the Chapter Coordinator before posting — quality control ensures only genuine, specific briefs reach the network.

### 2. Co-Development Sprints
Structured 90-day collaborations between a startup (solution builder), an NGO (implementation partner), and optionally a corporate (funder or technology contributor):

- **Week 1–2:** Problem definition and solution scoping workshop
- **Week 3–8:** Development and iteration (startup leads, NGO provides field insight)
- **Week 9–12:** Pilot test in real context; outcome documentation
- **Output:** A pilot project logged in the platform, outcomes documented, and a decision on whether to continue

The Chapter Coordinator facilitates and the partnership brokering methodology (see [[The-Partnering-Initiative]]) is applied throughout.

### 3. Demo Days
Quarterly internal showcases where startup members present their solutions to:
- NGO members who might be implementation partners
- Corporate members who might be clients or co-funders
- Philanthropist members who might provide grant funding

Demo Days are members-only — not a public pitch event. The trust network context means startups get genuine feedback and introductions, not polished audience reactions.

---

## Governance

The Innovation Hub is governed by a **Hub Committee** — a working group of the Cross-Chapter Council with representation from startup, NGO, and corporate member types. The Committee:
- Approves challenge briefs before posting
- Selects sprint participants (from applicants)
- Reviews sprint outcomes
- Designs Demo Day format each cycle

---

## Phase 1 Preparation

During Phase 1 (Year 1), the Hub is not formally launched but the groundwork is laid:
- At least 3 NGO members identified as potential challenge brief authors
- At least 3 startup members identified as potential sprint participants
- A simple challenge brief template developed and tested
- Chapter Coordinators trained in sprint facilitation basics
