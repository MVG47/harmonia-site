## Goal

Recruit 7–12 founding members for the UK chapter across all four member types, with a minimum of: 2 NGOs, 2 startups/social enterprises, 2 corporates, and 1 philanthropist/funder.

Founding members co-shape Harmonia's pledge language and governance. The pitch must reflect this: we are asking them to help build the network, not just join something that already exists.

---

## Approach: Credibility by Association

The UK social impact sector is large, mature, and well-networked — but also crowded with networks, coalitions, and platforms. Harmonia must immediately differentiate on what it actually requires of members (binding pledges, accountability) versus the typical "pay a fee, attend events" network model.

The strategy:

1. **Anchor relationship with Bond or BITC** — borrow their credibility to reach the NGO and corporate sectors respectively
2. **Soft launch event** at a credible venue (or co-hosted) — introduce Harmonia to 40–60 sector leaders
3. **Follow up with warm prospects** from the event and referrals
4. **Targeted direct outreach** to specific organisations that represent strategic gaps in the founding cohort

---

## Phase 1: Anchor Relationships (Month 1)

| Intermediary | Why them | What to propose |
|-------------|----------|-----------------|
| **Bond** | Membership body for 400+ UK international development NGOs; trusted voice; convenes sector heads regularly | Event partnership or endorsement; explore whether Bond members could be a primary founding member pipeline |
| **Social Enterprise UK** | Membership body for UK social enterprises; credibility with impact-driven startups and social entrepreneurs | Co-host a launch event or social enterprise founder roundtable |
| **Impact Investing Institute** | Cross-sector body connecting impact investment, corporates, and NGOs | Co-host an investor/funder roundtable; access to corporate impact and foundation leads |
| **Business in the Community (BITC)** | Largest responsible business network in UK; 700+ company members; convenes senior corporate social impact leaders | Explore whether a BITC introduction can open corporate founding member conversations |

---

## Phase 2: Soft Launch Event (Month 2)

A half-day or evening event in London introducing Harmonia to sector leaders.

**Format:** Provocative opening ("Why networks fail — and what a trust network does differently") + discussion + reception with structured conversations.

**Target attendees:** 40–60 individuals — heads of partnerships or strategy at NGOs, social enterprise founders, corporate social impact directors, and programme officers at foundations.

**Desired outcome:** 20+ warm prospect conversations; 8+ expressions of founding member interest.

**Venue/host:** Co-hosted with Bond (NGO credibility) or a neutral venue associated with Social Enterprise UK or Impact Hub London.

---

## Phase 3: Prospect Outreach by Sector

### NGO / Charity Prospects

**Criteria:** Active UK or international programming; genuine interest in cross-sector partnerships (not NGO-sector-only); leadership willing to make a real pledge commitment; willing to share impact data openly.

**Priority prospect categories:**
- A large UK-based international development NGO with an existing East Africa (Kenya) presence — creates immediate Kenya chapter linkage
- A mid-size NGO with a track record of corporate partnerships that has found them inadequate without a proper framework
- A domestic UK charity working in a theme (climate, digital inclusion, racial equity) where cross-sector partnerships are increasingly essential

**Entry pathway:** Bond membership directory; conversations at Bond events; introductions from Social Enterprise UK.

**Pitch emphasis:** Peer accountability and governance that prevents "network capture" by funders or corporates; reduced duplication with peer NGOs; access to vetted corporate and startup partners; collective voice on sector policy.

---

### Startup / Social Enterprise Prospects

**Criteria:** Social mission at the core (not a spin-off or marketing angle); working product or service; founder-led engagement preferred; interest in NGO partnerships as a core part of their growth strategy.

**Priority sectors:**
- **EdTech for social good:** digital learning platforms serving underserved communities
- **HealthTech:** tools for NHS, social care, or global health applications
- **Climate tech with social dimensions:** clean energy, sustainable food, waste reduction with demonstrable community impact
- **Financial inclusion / access:** tools addressing poverty, financial exclusion, or economic mobility
- **Civic tech:** transparency tools, community organising platforms, participatory decision-making

**Entry pathway:** Social Enterprise UK; Bethnal Green Ventures portfolio; Nesta contacts; School for Social Entrepreneurs alumni network.

**Pitch emphasis:** NGO implementation partners who can validate, test, and distribute their solutions; philanthropic capital access; corporate clients within the same trusted network; founding member status as an external validation of mission credibility.

---

### Corporate Prospects

**Criteria:** Genuine ESG/social impact leadership — not a marketing function; dedicated team with budget and senior sponsorship; no conflicts of interest with NGO members; willing to contribute time and expertise, not just funding.

**Priority organisations:**
- **Unilever** — long-standing sustainability leadership; supplier inclusion work; active NGO partnerships
- **Lloyds Banking Group / Lloyds Bank Foundation** — substantial social investment arm; financial inclusion alignment
- **HSBC** — HSBC Foundation and ESG commitments; global development programme
- **NatWest Group** — Entrepreneur Accelerator; financial inclusion; community lending
- **GSK / Haleon** — healthcare and access to medicine focus; social enterprise support
- **BT / EE** — digital inclusion programmes; strong community partnership history

**Entry pathway:** BITC's network (best single point of access to senior corporate social impact leads); introductions through NGO members who already have corporate partnerships; direct outreach to Foundation Directors or Heads of Social Impact.

**Pitch emphasis:** Network of vetted implementing partners with shared accountability standards; impact measurement framework that satisfies investor ESG disclosure requirements; skills-based volunteering structure; access to startups who can help them deliver their social commitments.

---

### Philanthropist / Funder Prospects

**Criteria:** Active grant-making in the UK or internationally; demonstrated interest in collaborative or trust-based approaches; programme officer or foundation director who will be personally present (not a passive donor relationship).

**Priority organisations:**
- **Joseph Rowntree Foundation** — systems change focus; open to collaborative approaches; domestic poverty and inequality
- **Paul Hamlyn Foundation** — arts and social change; trust-based giving ethos; relatively open to experimentation
- **Esmée Fairbairn Foundation** — civil society, environment, food; interested in cross-sector work
- **Comic Relief** — grant-making alongside UK and international NGOs; familiar with collaborative funding
- **Wellcome Trust** — health and wellbeing; large enough to co-fund collaborative mechanisms
- **Blagrave Trust** — youth-focused; trust-based philanthropy advocates; smaller but strategically aligned

**Entry pathway:** Association of Charitable Foundations (ACF) events; Trust and Foundation News network; Beacon Collaborative; warm introductions from NGO members who are existing grantees.

**Pitch emphasis:** Pooled funding mechanisms that leverage individual grants; built-in due diligence through member vetting; field intelligence from NGO and startup members; collaborative approach aligns with trust-based philanthropy principles many of these foundations are moving toward.
