## Overview

Phase 3 runs across Years 4–5. By this point Harmonia should be a self-sustaining trust network with proven cross-sector collaboration outcomes, 3+ active chapters, and a financial model that is not dependent on founding philanthropic grants. Phase 3 is about global reach, federated independence, and becoming the recognised infrastructure for cross-sector social impact collaboration worldwide.

**Entering Phase 3 requires:** Phase 2 targets met; third chapter approved; network financial sustainability at 80%+; Impact Hub proven with documented outcomes across at least 2 cohorts.

---

## Phase 3 Vision

> [!note] The Phase 3 aspiration
> By the end of Year 5, Harmonia is a globally recognised trust network with federated chapters across multiple geographies, a proven collective impact record, a self-sustaining revenue model, and a governance structure that is fully member-owned. The founding team's work is complete — the network runs itself.

## Year 4 Priorities

### Third chapter launch
- Third chapter formally launched with its own legal entity, elected committee, and founding cohort (minimum 10 organisations)
- Cross-Chapter Council expands to include third chapter with equal representation (3 seats)
- Shared platform, pledges, and governance framework applied without modification — no bespoke arrangements for new chapters

### Major cross-sector campaigns
- Design and launch the first network-wide campaign — a coordinated advocacy or collective action effort involving members from all chapters
- Campaign theme should address a challenge where Harmonia's cross-sector composition gives it a distinctive voice (e.g. closing the accountability gap in voluntary corporate social commitments, or cross-sector climate adaptation)
- Measure: media reach, policy engagement, member participation rate

### Federated financial model
- Each chapter is financially self-sustaining (membership fees + local earned revenue covers local costs)
- A small network-level fee (paid by each chapter to the cross-chapter entity) funds: the shared platform, the cross-chapter council secretariat, and the annual network convening
- External grants are used for innovation and expansion, not core operations

### Innovation Hub at scale
- Innovation Hub runs in all chapters, with at least one joint cross-chapter cohort per year
- Hub alumni network active — previous cohort participants become mentors and connectors
- Consider licensing the Innovation Hub methodology to partner organisations (earned revenue stream)

---

## Year 5 Priorities

### 4th and 5th chapter development
- Identify and begin feasibility assessment for Chapters 4 and 5
- Priority geographies assessed in Year 4; Cross-Chapter Council approval by Year 5
- Build a "chapter launch playbook" based on Kenya/UK/Chapter 3 experience — standardised but locally adaptable

### External evaluation and positioning
- Commission an independent external evaluation of Harmonia's collective impact (Years 1–5)
- Publish findings openly — including what didn't work
- Use evaluation evidence to position Harmonia as the leading example of trust-based cross-sector network infrastructure globally

### Governance maturity
- Harmonia's governance is fully member-owned; founding team plays no governance role
- Cross-Chapter Council has served multiple full election cycles
- Constitutional amendment process has been used at least once (demonstrates the process works)
- Member recall has been available as a right, even if never exercised

### Collective voice and policy influence
- Harmonia's cross-sector composition gives it a distinctive voice on policy issues where single-sector bodies lack credibility
- By Year 5: at least 2 documented cases where Harmonia's collective advocacy influenced a policy or practice change
- Relationships with key policy bodies in both Kenya (government ministries, KEPSA) and UK (FCDO, Cabinet Office Civil Society)

## What "Scale" Means (and Doesn't Mean)

Scale in Phase 3 does **not** mean maximising member count. Catalyst 2030 has 6,000+ members with no accountability layer. Harmonia's value proposition depends on the pledge being meaningful — which means the screening process and accountability mechanisms must work at scale.

Scale means:
- More chapters, each with a critical mass of genuinely engaged members
- Greater geographic and thematic diversity within the network
- Larger collective impact measurable through the shared KPI framework
- Financial sustainability without dependence on philanthropic goodwill

Scale does **not** mean:
- Lowering the membership bar to grow faster
- Reducing accountability mechanisms because they become harder to administer
- Centralising governance as the network grows (the federated model should strengthen, not weaken)

## Key Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|
| Network becomes too large to maintain trust culture | Chapter size limits (max 40–50 members per chapter); new chapters required before any single chapter over-concentrates |
| Financial self-sustainability not achieved by Year 5 | Year 3 sustainability target (80%) is a prerequisite; earned revenue development begins Phase 2 |
| Governance complexity with 5+ chapters | Cross-Chapter Council procedures codified and practiced; digital collaboration tools mature; possible dedicated network coordinator role |
| Founding team disengagement after governance handover | By design — the handover is the success metric, not a risk to mitigate |
