## Overview

Harmonia's six core principles are binding on all members. They are not aspirational values — they are the conditions of membership. Each principle has observable behaviours, and the [[Accountability-Mechanisms]] describe what happens when they are not met.

---

## 1. Transparency

**Definition:** Members openly share their activities, outcomes, challenges, and relevant organisational information with fellow members — not only their successes.

**What it looks like in practice:**
- Submitting an annual impact report to the network (required)
- Disclosing relevant conflicts of interest before partnership discussions
- Sharing lessons from failed projects, not just wins
- Making project data available to members where it doesn't breach privacy or confidentiality

**How it's verified:**
- Annual self-report reviewed by peer members
- Platform activity tracking (where applicable)
- Random spot checks as part of the accountability review cycle

**Why it matters:** Trust cannot be built on curated narratives. Harmonia's power comes from members actually knowing each other — which requires honest disclosure.

---

## 2. Accountability

**Definition:** Members commit to measurable goals, report honestly on progress, and accept the consequences when they fall short.

**What it looks like in practice:**
- Setting specific, measurable commitments when joining or at each annual review
- Submitting timely reports rather than going silent when things are difficult
- Responding constructively to peer feedback and accountability reviews
- Engaging with support mechanisms before consequences escalate

**How it's verified:**
- Commitment tracking on the Unified Impact Platform
- Annual review process with chapter committee sign-off
- Peer accountability circles (small groups of 4–6 members who review each other)

**Why it matters:** Accountability that relies on goodwill alone fails. Harmonia's graduated consequences framework gives accountability teeth — supportively, before punitively.

---

## 3. Collaboration

**Definition:** Members actively participate in shared initiatives and support fellow members — the network is something they contribute to, not just draw from.

**What it looks like in practice:**
- Participating in at least one cross-sector initiative per year
- Responding to resource requests from fellow members within 30 days
- Offering in-kind support (expertise, networks, platforms) as well as financial
- Joining at least one working group or thematic community of practice

**How it's verified:**
- Platform engagement data
- Annual participation self-report
- Cross-member referencing (members can vouch for — or flag — each other's engagement)

**Why it matters:** A network of members who only attend events and receive benefits is a directory, not a trust network. Harmonia works because members put in as well as take out.

---

## 4. Equity

**Definition:** All partners are treated fairly regardless of organisational size, sector, or geography. Grassroots organisations have genuine voice alongside large institutions.

**What it looks like in practice:**
- One member, one vote on network decisions (regardless of size or fee tier)
- Governance structures that give chapter committees — not just a central body — real authority
- Subsidised membership for qualifying grassroots organisations and early-stage startups
- Deliberate inclusion of small/local organisations in working groups and convenings

**How it's verified:**
- Annual governance review assessing representation across sectors and geographies
- Membership demographic reporting (organisation size, type, region)
- Member satisfaction survey disaggregated by organisation type

**Why it matters:** Networks that claim equity but concentrate power in large organisations quickly lose the trust of smaller partners — and lose their most important implementation knowledge.

---

## 5. Impact First

**Definition:** Members prioritise genuine social outcomes over organisational self-interest, reputation, or short-term gain.

**What it looks like in practice:**
- Being honest about what your organisation can and cannot deliver
- Referring partners to other Harmonia members when they're better placed to help
- Not using the network primarily as a marketing or profile-raising platform
- Aligning participation with genuine programmatic priorities, not just optics

**How it's verified:**
- Annual reflection on whether organisational goals and network contributions are genuinely aligned
- Peer review within accountability circles
- Monitoring of platform use patterns for extractive behaviour

**Why it matters:** Networks fail when members extract reputational benefit without contributing. "Impact First" is the cultural norm that prevents Harmonia from becoming a badge collectors' club.

---

## 6. Continuous Improvement

**Definition:** Members commit to learning, adapting their practice, and actively sharing what works — and what doesn't — with the wider network.

**What it looks like in practice:**
- Participating in learning events, peer exchanges, and innovation programming
- Contributing at least one case study or learning brief to the network per year
- Actively seeking out best practices from other sectors and geographies
- Updating approaches based on evidence and feedback

**How it's verified:**
- Knowledge contribution tracking (case studies, forum posts, event participation)
- Annual learning reflection as part of the impact report

**Why it matters:** The social impact sector makes slow progress in part because organisations rarely share failures or learning. Harmonia is designed to be a genuine learning network, not just a collaboration platform.
