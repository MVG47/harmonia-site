## What Resource Pooling Is

One of Harmonia's core value propositions is that members access resources they could never afford independently — pro bono legal advice, data expertise, venue space, technology platforms, field research capacity. Resource pooling is the mechanism that makes this happen in a trackable, reciprocal way.

Resource pooling is distinct from partnership (which involves two organisations working together on a project). It is a lighter-touch contribution: one member offers something, another member uses it, both log it.

---

## What Counts as a Resource

| Resource type | Examples |
|--------------|---------|
| **Expertise** | Legal advice, financial modelling, impact measurement design, communications strategy, technology architecture |
| **Staff time** | Secondments, pro bono project work, mentoring, training delivery |
| **Networks** | Introductions to funders, government contacts, sector leaders |
| **Technology / Platforms** | Access to software tools, data platforms, digital infrastructure |
| **Physical space** | Meeting rooms, event venues, coworking access |
| **Financial** | Micro-grants, matched funding contributions, emergency support |
| **In-kind goods** | Equipment, materials, printed resources |

## How It Works

### Offering a resource
A member posts an offer to the Resource Pool:
- What's being offered (type and description)
- Estimated value (hours or £/KES — for network reporting)
- Who it's available to (any member / specific sector / specific thematic area)
- How long the offer is open

### Requesting a resource
A member posts a resource request:
- What they need and why
- Timeline
- What they can offer in return (optional but encouraged)

Chapter Coordinators facilitate matches where the platform doesn't make them obvious.

### Logging a contribution
Once a resource is used, both the contributing and receiving member log it in the platform:
- What was provided
- Estimated value
- Outcome (if known)

This data feeds KPI 4 (resources mobilised through the network).

---

## Reciprocity Norm

Resource pooling should feel reciprocal over time — members who receive should also contribute. This is tracked softly (Coordinators notice if a member only receives and never contributes) and addressed through the peer accountability circle if it becomes persistent.

There is no requirement for equal exchange in a single transaction — a small NGO receiving pro bono legal advice from a corporate is not expected to offer equivalent value back immediately. Reciprocity operates at the network level over time, not transaction by transaction.
